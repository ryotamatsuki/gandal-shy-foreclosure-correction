\begin{abstract}
International product-standard agreements can foreclose foreign suppliers, but exclusion from equilibrium sales need not eliminate their effect on market prices. Gandal and Shy (2001) correctly identify $c=5/2$ as the foreclosure threshold in their quadratic-transport model of a standardization union, yet their post-foreclosure price characterization does not hold over the full parameter range. Re-solving the price subgame yields $p_M=c-1$ for $5/2<c<3$, where the excluded foreign firm imposes a binding entry constraint and member firms engage in limit pricing. For $3\leq c<5$, that constraint is slack and the unconstrained symmetric duopoly price $p_M=2$ obtains. Recomputing national welfare under the corrected equilibrium leaves the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 unchanged. The correction therefore distinguishes foreclosure of realized sales from the disappearance of competitive discipline while establishing the robustness of the original welfare conclusion.
\end{abstract}
