\begin{abstract}
Foreclosure is an allocation outcome, but it need not make an excluded rival strategically irrelevant. This note revisits the post-foreclosure price equilibrium in the standardization-union model of Gandal and Shy (2001). In the published quadratic-transport specification, $c=5/2$ correctly marks the point at which the outsider's equilibrium demand falls to zero, but the reported constant post-foreclosure price does not characterize the Nash equilibrium above that boundary. Re-solving the price subgame yields two regimes. For $5/2<c<3$, member firms set $p_M=c-1$: the outsider makes no sales, yet a higher member price would restore its demand, so the exclusion constraint binds and the members engage in limit pricing. For $3\leq c<5$, the constraint is slack and the unconstrained symmetric duopoly price $p_M=2$ obtains. The corrected model therefore moves from three-firm competition to a binding-exclusion limit-pricing regime and then to unconstrained duopoly. Recomputing national welfare under the corrected equilibrium leaves the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 unchanged because the relevant price transfers cancel. The contribution is a narrow equilibrium correction with an industrial-organization interpretation and a robustness result.
\end{abstract}
