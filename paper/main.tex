\begin{abstract}
International product standards can shape cross-border market access by changing the conversion costs faced by foreign firms. This note revisits the post-foreclosure price equilibrium in the standardization-union model of Gandal and Shy (2001). In the published quadratic-transport specification, $c=5/2$ correctly marks the point at which the outsider's equilibrium demand falls to zero, but the reported constant post-foreclosure price is not a Nash equilibrium throughout the foreclosure region. Re-solving the price subgame yields two regimes. For $5/2<c<3$, member firms set $p_M=c-1$: the outsider makes no sales, yet a higher member price would restore its demand, so the exclusion constraint binds and members engage in limit pricing. For $3\leq c<5$, the constraint is slack and the unconstrained symmetric duopoly price $p_M=2$ obtains. The corrected benchmark therefore moves from three-firm competition to binding-exclusion limit pricing and then to unconstrained duopoly. Recomputing national welfare under the corrected equilibrium leaves the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 unchanged because the relevant price transfers cancel. Thus the correction changes the strategic implementation of the standardization-union regime while confirming the robustness of its original welfare comparison.
\end{abstract}
