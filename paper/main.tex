\begin{abstract}
International product standards can shape cross-border market access by changing the conversion costs faced by foreign firms. This note revisits the post-foreclosure price subgame in the standardization-union model of Gandal and Shy (2001). The published quadratic-transport specification correctly places the outsider-foreclosure threshold at $c=5/2$, but Appendix B uses an inconsistent long-arc price response and its claimed complete price profile is not Nash in the strict foreclosure range. Correcting the geometry does not yield a unique replacement price. Within symmetric, foreclosed, pure-strategy profiles, the unrestricted price game admits a continuum of equilibria, with member prices from $3/2$ up to $\min\{2,c-1\}$ subject to the corresponding outsider-price conditions. If prices are instead explicitly restricted to be no lower than marginal cost, the member price within the same equilibrium class is $c-1$ for $5/2<c<3$ and $2$ for $3\leq c<5$. This is a separate restricted game, not a condition imposed in the published model. Under a common symmetric continuation across the two union markets, member-country welfare remains $3V+1/4$, one half above mutual recognition; under market-specific continuation prices, a cross-market transfer term remains. The correction therefore changes the equilibrium correspondence and makes the welfare comparison continuation-selection dependent.
\end{abstract}
