\begin{abstract}
This note revisits the post-foreclosure price equilibrium in Gandal and Shy (2001). Their published quadratic-transport model correctly identifies $c=5/2$ as the threshold at which an outsider is foreclosed from a standardization-union member market, but re-solving the two-active-firm subgame changes subsequent prices. Member firms charge $p_M=c-1$ for $5/2<c<3$, where the foreclosure constraint binds, and $p_M=2$ for $3\leq c<5$, the unconstrained duopoly price. The first interval is therefore a limit-pricing regime. Recomputing welfare under the corrected equilibrium shows that the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 remains valid because consumer price payments and member-firm revenues offset in national welfare.
\end{abstract}
