\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The standardization-union benchmark of Gandal and Shy (2001) has the correct foreclosure threshold but an incorrect post-foreclosure price characterization in its published quadratic-transport specification. Outsider demand reaches zero at $c=5/2$, as in the original analysis. Once foreclosure occurs, however, member firms charge $p_M=c-1$ for $5/2<c<3$ and $p_M=2$ for $3\leq c<5$.

This piecewise equilibrium changes how the standards regime is implemented in product-market competition. In the intermediate region the outsider has zero equilibrium sales but remains a binding competitive constraint: a higher member price would restore its demand. The standardization union therefore moves from three-firm competition to binding-exclusion limit pricing and only then to unconstrained duopoly. Realized exclusion of a foreign firm need not coincide with the disappearance of its competitive pressure.

Recomputing national welfare under the corrected equilibrium leaves the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 unchanged. The corrected prices alter consumer expenditure, producer profit, and the distribution of surplus, but the relevant price terms cancel in the original national-welfare comparison between the standardization-union outcome and mutual recognition. The correction therefore changes the equilibrium mechanism while confirming the robustness of that particular policy comparison; it does not imply a general welfare-invariance result for standards agreements.
