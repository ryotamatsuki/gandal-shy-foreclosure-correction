\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The foreclosure threshold in Gandal and Shy (2001) is unchanged: the outsider's three-firm interior demand in a standardization-union member market falls to zero at $c=5/2$. The post-foreclosure price equilibrium implied by the published quadratic transportation technology is different from the constant member price reported in Appendix B. Correcting the long-arc demand yields two post-foreclosure regimes. For $5/2<c<3$, the foreclosure constraint binds and member firms charge $p_M=c-1$, so exclusion is sustained by limit pricing. For $3\leq c<5$, the constraint is slack and the member price is the unconstrained duopoly price $2$.

Recomputing welfare under these prices leaves the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 intact. The corrected member price changes consumer expenditure and producer profit, but the corresponding price terms offset in national welfare across the symmetric union markets. The correction therefore changes the equilibrium characterization and the economic path from three-firm competition to duopoly, while leaving the headline welfare comparison for union members unchanged. This distinction is the central point of the note: the product-market mechanism requires correction even though the final member-welfare ranking is robust.
