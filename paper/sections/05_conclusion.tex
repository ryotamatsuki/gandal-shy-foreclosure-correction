\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The post-foreclosure calculation in Gandal and Shy (2001) is inconsistent with the quadratic transportation primitive used in the published model. Correcting the long-arc boundary shows that the complete Appendix-B profile $(3/2,3/2,c)$ is not Nash in the strict foreclosure range. The appropriate replacement is not a single unrestricted price branch: within symmetric, foreclosed, pure-strategy profiles, the original price game admits a continuum of member prices supported by different zero-sales outsider quotes.

A separate benchmark explicitly rules out below-cost prices. Within the same symmetric foreclosed class, this strategy restriction yields the piecewise member price $c-1$ for $5/2<c<3$ and $2$ for $3\leq c<5$. The lower branch illustrates how an outsider with zero sales can still constrain active-firm pricing, but the restriction modifies the strategy set rather than restating a condition from the original paper.

The welfare implication is correspondingly conditional. A common symmetric continuation across the two union markets reproduces the original member-country gap of $1/2$ over mutual recognition, whereas different unrestricted continuation prices leave a cross-market transfer term. The correction therefore changes both the equilibrium correspondence and the scope of the welfare-robustness claim. It does not establish a government-stage policy reversal; it identifies precisely the continuation conditions under which the original comparison is preserved.
