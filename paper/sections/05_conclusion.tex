\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This note revisits the post-foreclosure price subgame in the quadratic-transport model of Gandal and Shy (2001). The foreclosure threshold itself is unchanged: outsider demand in a union member market reaches zero at $c=5/2$. What changes is the equilibrium after that point. For $5/2<c<3$, the excluded foreign firm still constrains member pricing because a price increase would restore its demand, so the member firms limit-price at $p_M=c-1$. For $3\leq c<5$, the outsider constraint is slack and the unconstrained symmetric duopoly price $p_M=2$ obtains.

The economic distinction is between foreclosure of realized sales and disappearance of competitive discipline. A foreign supplier can have zero equilibrium sales while its potential return to positive demand remains a binding constraint on domestic pricing. Only when conversion costs become sufficiently high does that constraint cease to matter.

Recomputing welfare under the corrected equilibrium leaves the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 intact. Corrected prices redistribute surplus between consumers and the domestic producer, but the relevant price terms offset in national welfare across the symmetric union markets. The equilibrium correction therefore sharpens the mechanism of foreclosure in the model while establishing that its principal member-country welfare conclusion is robust to the corrected pricing subgame.
