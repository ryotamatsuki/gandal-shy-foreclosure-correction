\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The foreclosure threshold in Gandal and Shy (2001) is unchanged: outsider demand in a standardization union member market reaches zero at $c=5/2$. What changes is the Nash equilibrium after foreclosure. In the published quadratic-transport specification, the members charge $p_M=c-1$ for $5/2<c<3$, when the exclusion constraint binds, and $p_M=2$ for $3\leq c<5$, when that constraint is slack and the unconstrained symmetric duopoly price is feasible.

The intermediate region has a direct industrial-organization interpretation. The outsider has zero equilibrium sales but remains a binding competitive constraint because a higher member price would restore its demand. Realized foreclosure therefore does not coincide with strategic irrelevance in this parameter range. Only once $c$ reaches three does the outsider cease to constrain the members' unconstrained pricing incentives.

Recomputing welfare under the corrected equilibrium leaves the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 intact. Corrected prices change consumer expenditure, producer profit, and the distribution of surplus, but the corresponding price terms offset in the national-welfare calculation used in the original proposition. The correction thus changes the equilibrium mechanism while establishing the robustness of that particular welfare comparison. The example illustrates, without claiming a general theorem, that foreclosure in realized sales need not coincide with the disappearance of a rival's competitive constraint.
