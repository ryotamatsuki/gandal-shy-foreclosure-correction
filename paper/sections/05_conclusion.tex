\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The foreclosure threshold in Gandal and Shy (2001) is unchanged: outsider demand in a union member market reaches zero at $c=5/2$. The post-foreclosure equilibrium implied by the published quadratic transportation technology is different from the constant price reported in Appendix B. For $5/2<c<3$, the foreclosure constraint binds and member firms charge $p_M=c-1$; for $3\leq c<5$, the constraint is slack and the unconstrained duopoly price $2$ obtains.

Recomputing welfare under the corrected equilibrium leaves the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 intact. The price correction changes consumer expenditure and producer profit, but the corresponding terms offset in national welfare across the symmetric union markets. The correction therefore changes the equilibrium characterization and inserts a limit-pricing phase between three-firm competition and unconstrained duopoly, while leaving the headline member-country welfare comparison unchanged.
