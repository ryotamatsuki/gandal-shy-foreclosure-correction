\section{Welfare robustness}\label{sec:welfare}

The equilibrium correction changes prices, consumer expenditure, and member-firm profits throughout the foreclosure region. It does not, however, overturn the member-country welfare ranking reported in Proposition 3 of Gandal and Shy (2001). This is not because the corrected prices happen to be numerically close to the published price. For example, the member price rises with $c$ over $5/2<c<3$ and equals $2$ thereafter. The ranking survives because the relevant price terms cancel once national welfare is recomputed consistently under the corrected two-firm allocation.

Take either union country, say country 1. Under foreclosure the two active member firms each sell $3/2$ units in that market. With equal prices, the market boundaries lie at the midpoints of the short and long arcs. Each firm serves one half-arc of length $1/2$ and one half-arc of length $1$. Total transportation cost is therefore
\begin{align}\label{eq:transport-foreclosure}
 T^{\SU}
 &=2\int_{0}^{1/2}x^2\,dx+2\int_{0}^{1}x^2\,dx \\
 &=\frac34.
\end{align}
Since aggregate demand is three and the common member price is $p_M$, consumers spend $3p_M$. Their gross consumption value is $3V$, so consumer surplus in a member country is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cs-su}
 CS_M^{\SU}=3V-3p_M-\frac34.
\end{equation}
This expression differs from the numerical expenditure calculation in the published Appendix C whenever the corrected member price differs from $3/2$.

National welfare also includes the worldwide profit of the country's domestic firm. That firm sells $3/2$ units in each of the two union markets. Because production cost is zero for a member firm in those markets, its combined union-market profit is $3p_M$. Under the outsider-country recognition behavior used in the proof of Gandal and Shy's Proposition 3, all standards are recognized in the nonmember country, where the member firm earns one additional unit of profit \citep[p.~382]{gandalshy2001}. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profit-su}
 \Pi_M^{\SU}=3p_M+1.
\end{equation}
The same corrected price that lowers consumer surplus therefore raises the domestic firm's union-market profit by an exactly offsetting amount.

\begin{proposition}[Welfare robustness]\label{prop:welfare}
Under the recognition behavior considered in Proposition 3 of Gandal and Shy (2001), the corrected foreclosure equilibrium yields, for either standardization-union member,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ts-su}
 TS_M^{\SU}=3V+\frac14
\end{equation}
for $5/2<c<5$. Mutual recognition yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ts-mr}
 TS^{\MR}=3V-\frac14,
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:welfare-gap}
 TS_M^{\SU}-TS^{\MR}=\frac12>0.
\end{equation}
Hence the member-country welfare ranking in Gandal and Shy's Proposition 3 survives the correction of the post-foreclosure price equilibrium.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equations \eqref{eq:cs-su} and \eqref{eq:profit-su} imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 TS_M^{\SU}
 &=CS_M^{\SU}+\Pi_M^{\SU}\\
 &=3V-3p_M-\frac34+3p_M+1\\
 &=3V+\frac14.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This calculation holds for either branch of Proposition \ref{prop:equilibrium}; it requires the foreclosed two-firm allocation and the recognition behavior just described, but not the particular value of $p_M$ within the corrected equilibrium.

For comparison, under mutual recognition all standards are accepted in every country and all three firms are symmetric. In a representative national market each firm charges $1$ and sells one unit, so consumers spend $3$. With three evenly spaced active firms, transportation cost is $1/4$, giving consumer surplus $3V-13/4$. The domestic firm earns one unit in each of the three segmented national markets, for worldwide profit $3$. National total surplus is therefore
\[
 TS^{\MR}=3V-\frac{13}{4}+3=3V-\frac14.
\]
Subtracting from \eqref{eq:ts-su} yields \eqref{eq:welfare-gap}.
\end{proof}

The welfare result separates two questions that are easy to conflate. Proposition \ref{prop:equilibrium} concerns strategic pricing and is sensitive to the correct long-arc demand slope. Proposition \ref{prop:welfare} concerns national surplus after summing consumer surplus and domestic-firm profit across segmented markets. The second calculation does not undo the first. Rather, the corrected price redistributes surplus between consumers and the domestic producer inside the symmetric union: consumers in one member country pay $3p_M$, while that country's firm earns $3p_M$ from its combined sales in the two member markets. Those terms cancel in national welfare.

What remains after the cancellation is economically meaningful but price-independent within this particular comparison: the transportation loss generated by serving the member market with two firms and the member firm's unit profit in the recognizing outsider market. The robustness result should therefore not be read as a general invariance theorem for standardization unions. It is a property of the accounting and symmetry in the environment used for Proposition 3. The correction changes the equilibrium mechanism and the distribution of surplus, but not the headline member-country ranking.
