\section{Technical details and secondary corrections}\label{app:technical}

\subsection{Boundary and zero-demand price details}
At $c=5/2$, the three-active-firm solution gives $p_M=3/2$ and $q_O=0$, matching the limit-pricing branch $p_M=c-1$. At $c=3$, the limit-pricing price $c-1$ equals the unconstrained duopoly price $2$. For $c\geq3$, the member allocation can be supported by a range of zero-demand outsider prices; the main text therefore does not claim uniqueness of an economically irrelevant zero-sales outsider price.

\subsection{Table 2 arithmetic correction}
Recomputing the first conversion-cost row of Table 2 gives member-country surplus
\[
 3V+\frac{20c^2-60c-25}{100},
\]
where the published table has coefficient $16c^2$. The difference is $c^2/25$. This is an off-equilibrium row for the outsider's recognition decision and does not alter Proposition 2's equilibrium comparison.

\subsection{Country size}
The member firm's per-member-market profit coefficient is piecewise once foreclosure is imposed:
\[
 m(c)=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{(c+5)^2}{25}, & 0<c<\dfrac52,\\[4pt]
 \dfrac32(c-1), & \dfrac52<c<3,\\[4pt]
 3, & c\geq3.
 \end{cases}
\]
In every relevant positive-$c$ region $m(c)>1$, so the qualitative ranking that a member prefers a larger union partner survives even though the interior profit formula cannot be extended through the foreclosure region.

\subsection{Network-effect analogue}
The same long-arc geometry applies when foreclosure is generated by a network-utility advantage rather than conversion cost. Writing the relevant network parameter as $d$, the corrected member price is $p_M=d-1$ for $5/2<d<3$ and $p_M=2$ for $d\geq3$. Recomputing member-country welfare preserves the original qualitative comparison in which mutual recognition dominates the standardization union in the network-effect case. This observation is reported only as a consistency check and is not an additional proposition.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
The symbolic identities used in Propositions \ref{prop:equilibrium} and \ref{prop:welfare}, together with numerical best-response checks around the regime boundaries, are reproduced by the scripts in the repository's \texttt{code/} directory.
