## 4. Publication-significance audit

### 4.1 Is this more than an algebraic corrigendum?

**Yes.**

The paper should not be positioned as merely correcting the long-arc coefficient from `1/2` to `1/4`. That algebraic error is the trigger, but the scientific consequence is larger:

- the complete price profile claimed as the unique Bertrand–Nash equilibrium is not Nash;
- the unrestricted original game instead has a nondegenerate continuum of symmetric foreclosed equilibria;
- the outsider's zero-sales quote affects the members' global best-response problem even when the outsider sells nothing;
- the welfare comparison becomes continuation-selection conditional.

This is an equilibrium-structure correction.

### 4.2 Publication-significance classification

The revised contribution is classified as follows:

| Category | Stage 12R2 assessment |
|---|---|
| Typographical correction | No |
| Local algebraic correction only | No |
| Nondegenerate equilibrium-regime / equilibrium-set correction | **Yes** |
| Economically meaningful strategic-mechanism correction | **Yes, within the stated symmetric foreclosed class** |
| Policy conclusion reversed | No |
| Policy conclusion made selection-dependent | **Yes** |
| New primitive / institutional extension | No |
| Full unrestricted government-stage solution | No |

The strongest publication value is therefore not a new policy prescription. It is the correction of a published equilibrium and uniqueness claim, the resulting multiplicity, and the clarification of what is and is not robust in the associated welfare comparison.

### 4.3 Strongest acceptance argument

A benchmark international-standardization model makes a published uniqueness claim for the continuation price game. Under the model's own quadratic transport primitive, the stated profile is not Nash. Correcting the geometry reveals that the unrestricted game has a continuum of symmetric foreclosed equilibria because a zero-sales outsider can post a below-cost quote that changes active firms' global deviation incentives. This changes the strategic interpretation of foreclosure and makes the original welfare comparison conditional on continuation selection. The paper then shows transparently how an explicit no-below-cost strategy restriction recovers the piecewise member-price branch within a clearly stated equilibrium class.

That package is substantially more informative than an erratum that replaces one formula.

### 4.4 Strongest desk-reject argument

The benchmark is from 2001; the paper does not introduce a new primitive; it does not characterize all asymmetric or mixed equilibria; it does not solve the government stage under unrestricted continuation multiplicity; and it does not show that the original policy ranking actually reverses in equilibrium. An editor could therefore view the result as a technically correct but specialist correction whose downstream impact is uncertain.

This remains a real desk-reject risk.

### 4.5 Which argument wins?

**The acceptance argument is strong enough to justify submission, but not strong enough to make acceptance likely.**

The key change from the first Stage 12R is that the paper no longer says merely "the price should be `c-1` or `2`, but welfare is unchanged." It says that the published claimed unique continuation is false, unrestricted equilibrium is genuinely multiple, a transparent strategy restriction changes the equilibrium set, and the welfare statement itself depends on continuation selection.

That is a defensible standalone short-paper contribution.

No additional model extension is authorized at Stage 12R2 solely to improve perceived significance. Such an extension would create new technical risk and undermine the purpose of a correction note.

## 5. Does the symmetric-foreclosed equilibrium scope create a fatal editorial problem?

**No, provided the manuscript states the quantifier prominently and does not imply global completeness.**

The paper does not need to characterize all asymmetric and mixed equilibria to refute the published complete profile or its uniqueness claim. One profitable deviation is sufficient to refute the published profile, and the continuum of symmetric foreclosed equilibria is sufficient to refute the claimed uniqueness of the continuation in the class the original argument treats symmetrically.

The restricted scope becomes an editorial problem only if the manuscript tries to infer one of the following:

- that the complete unrestricted equilibrium correspondence has been solved;
- that the cost-floor game has a globally unique Nash equilibrium;
- that Proposition 3 reverses at the government stage;
- that all possible continuation selections have been ranked.

C3R/C4R already prohibit these statements.

Stage 13R2 must make the theorem class visible in Proposition 1 itself, not only in a footnote.

## 6. Role of the cost-floor result

The explicit strategy restriction

`p_i >= marginal cost in that market`

should remain in the paper, but its role changes.

It should **not** be the primary correction and should **not** be sold as an equilibrium refinement that is WLOG or uniquely compelling.

Its correct role is:

1. first solve the unrestricted original game accurately enough to expose multiplicity;
2. then show that a simple and economically transparent no-below-cost strategy restriction removes the below-cost zero-sales supports and yields the previously derived piecewise member price within symmetric foreclosed pure strategies;
3. use this restricted game as one transparent benchmark under which the original common-price welfare comparison survives.

This makes the cost-floor result useful as a disciplined comparative benchmark rather than an ad hoc device for deleting inconvenient equilibria.

**Stage 12R2 instruction:** retain the cost-floor result in Proposition 1, but subordinate it to the unrestricted-game correction and multiplicity result.
