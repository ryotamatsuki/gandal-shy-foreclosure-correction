## Referee tests

### 1. Why should a 2026 reader care about a correction to a 2001 paper?

**PASS.** The correction concerns a benchmark international-standards model and changes the strategic path over a nondegenerate parameter interval. It establishes that exclusion from realized foreign sales need not eliminate the excluded firm's pricing constraint. The framing is explicitly tied to standards recognition, market access, and international competition rather than to algebraic error hunting.

### 2. Is this merely an algebraic correction?

**PASS.** The published post-foreclosure profile fails Nash-equilibrium incentives. Correcting the long-arc demand response creates a distinct binding-foreclosure/limit-pricing regime for `5/2<c<3` and an unconstrained duopoly regime for `3<=c<5`.

### 3. Does the corrected result change an economically meaningful conclusion?

**PASS.** It changes equilibrium prices and the mechanism of foreign competitive discipline after foreclosure. It does not reverse the headline welfare ranking, and the manuscript states that distinction explicitly.

### 4. If the welfare ranking is unchanged, is the paper still worth publishing?

**WEAK PASS.** The value is the combination of a corrected Nash equilibrium, a missing strategic regime, and an independent demonstration that the original welfare ranking survives for a structural accounting reason. This remains the main editorial-risk point.

### 5. Is the limit-pricing interpretation genuinely supported by equilibrium incentives?

**PASS.** For `5/2<c<3`, a member price above `c-1` restores outsider demand; the outsider's constraint binds at the candidate equilibrium. Global member deviations and outsider deviations were checked analytically and numerically. The paper does not claim limit pricing itself as novel.

### 6. Is the paper sufficiently connected to international economics rather than only IO?

**PASS.** The institutional object is cross-border product-standard recognition; the mechanism concerns a foreign outsider's market access and pricing constraint; the welfare comparison is between a standardization union and mutual recognition. The revised introduction also positions the note within the international standards literature, including two RIE papers.

### 7. Is the manuscript self-contained?

**PASS.** Primitives, foreclosure threshold, post-foreclosure demand, equilibrium proof, and welfare recomputation are all in the main text. The reader does not need the original paper to verify the two propositions.

### 8. Could the contribution be explained in fewer pages?

**PASS.** The RIE manuscript is 12 double-spaced pages including references and declarations, well below the journal's 25-page limit. Further compression would mainly remove the global-deviation proof or economic interpretation and would weaken auditability.

## Desk-reject simulation

An editor reading only the title, abstract, first page, proposition statements, and conclusion sees:

1. an international-standards and market-access question;
2. a corrected equilibrium over a nondegenerate parameter interval;
3. a zero-sales foreign firm that remains a binding competitive constraint;
4. a transition from limit pricing to unconstrained duopoly;
5. a re-established, rather than assumed, original welfare ranking.

The strongest desk-reject argument remains: the underlying paper is old, the correction is narrow, and the headline welfare conclusion survives. The revised manuscript does not attempt to solve this by adding an artificial extension.
