# RIO Before / After Summary

## Title

**Before**

*Foreclosure and Limit Pricing in Standardization Unions: A Correction to Gandal and Shy (2001)*

**RIO version**

*Foreclosure and Limit Pricing in Standardization Unions: Revisiting Gandal and Shy (2001)*

Rationale: retain the original-paper connection while avoiding confusion with a publisher-issued Correction notice.

## Abstract

**Before**

Opened directly with the fact that the note revisits Gandal and Shy and then reported the corrected piecewise prices. The economic mechanism was present but secondary, and the abstract was below RIO's 150-word minimum.

**RIO version**

Opens with the IO distinction between foreclosure as an allocation outcome and strategic irrelevance. It then presents the correction, the binding exclusion constraint, the transition to unconstrained duopoly, and welfare robustness. The revised abstract is within RIO's 150–250 word requirement.

## Introduction opening

**Before**

Opened with national recognition of foreign product standards, conversion costs, network effects, and the subsequent trade-policy literature.

**RIO version**

Opens with the product-market pricing question: a zero-sales excluded rival may remain strategically relevant if an incumbent price increase restores profitable demand. The standardization union is introduced as the application rather than as the main contribution.

## Contribution statement

**Before**

The correction was framed primarily as re-solving the post-foreclosure price subgame in an international-standards model and confirming the original welfare ranking.

**RIO version**

The contribution is explicitly three-part and narrow:

1. correct the post-foreclosure Nash equilibrium;
2. identify the omitted nondegenerate binding-exclusion / limit-pricing regime;
3. establish that the original member-country welfare ranking is robust to the corrected pricing equilibrium.

No general novelty is claimed for foreclosure or limit pricing.

## Economic interpretation

**Before**

The manuscript stated that the zero-sales outsider disciplines member prices in the intermediate region.

**RIO version**

That statement becomes the organizing IO mechanism: realized foreclosure does not imply disappearance of the rival's competitive constraint. The manuscript also distinguishes the mechanism from informational signaling models of classic limit pricing.

## Conclusion

**Before**

Emphasized that the price correction changes the equilibrium and inserts a limit-pricing phase while leaving the headline welfare comparison unchanged.

**RIO version**

Emphasizes the strategic transition from a zero-sales but binding rival constraint to unconstrained duopoly. Welfare invariance is presented as a separate robustness result arising from price-transfer cancellation in the specific national-welfare accounting.
