Please consider the manuscript, “Foreclosure and Limit Pricing in Standardization Unions: Revisiting Gandal and Shy (2001),” for publication in Review of International Economics.

The paper revisits a price subgame in Gandal and Shy (2001), a model of international standards recognition, market access, and trade. The published quadratic-transport model correctly identifies c=5/2 as the threshold at which a foreign outsider is foreclosed from a standardization-union member market. Re-solving the post-foreclosure subgame shows that exclusion from realized sales does not immediately remove the foreign firm's competitive constraint. For 5/2<c<3, member firms charge p_M=c-1 because a higher price would restore outsider demand, producing a limit-pricing regime. For 3<=c<5, this constraint is slack and the unconstrained symmetric duopoly price p_M=2 obtains.

The correction changes the equilibrium characterization over a nondegenerate parameter interval but does not reverse the original member-country welfare ranking. Recomputing national welfare under the corrected equilibrium gives the same ranking as Proposition 3 because the relevant consumer-payment and domestic-profit terms offset. The paper therefore provides both a corrected equilibrium mechanism and a robustness result.

The manuscript fits Review of International Economics through its focus on product-standard recognition, foreign-firm foreclosure, market access, and the welfare comparison between a standardization union and mutual recognition. It is self-contained and deliberately concise; no new model or empirical extension is introduced. Symbolic and numerical verification scripts are supplied as supporting information.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no conflict of interest and no specific research funding. The AI-use declaration and data-availability statement are included with the manuscript and submission materials.
