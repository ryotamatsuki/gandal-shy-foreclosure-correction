Please consider the manuscript, “Foreclosure and Limit Pricing in Standardization Unions: Revisiting Gandal and Shy (2001),” for publication in the Review of Industrial Organization. The journal’s explicit interest in shorter notes and commentaries makes it a natural outlet for this concise correction of a published oligopoly equilibrium.

The note revisits the post-foreclosure price subgame in Gandal and Shy (2001). The published quadratic-transport specification correctly identifies c=5/2 as the point at which the outsider’s equilibrium demand in a standardization-union member market reaches zero. Re-solving the subsequent price game, however, reveals an omitted equilibrium constraint. For 5/2<c<3, member firms set p_M=c-1: the outsider sells nothing, but a higher member price would restore its demand, so the exclusion constraint binds and the members engage in limit pricing. For 3<=c<5, that constraint is slack and the unconstrained symmetric duopoly price p_M=2 obtains.

The correction is therefore not a change in primitives or a new model. It is a correction of the Nash equilibrium over a nondegenerate parameter interval, with a direct industrial-organization interpretation: realized foreclosure need not imply that an excluded rival has ceased to discipline incumbent pricing. The note also recomputes national welfare under the corrected equilibrium and shows that the member-country ranking in Proposition 3 remains valid because the relevant price transfers cancel.

The manuscript is self-contained, contains two propositions, and is accompanied by symbolic and numerical verification code. The work is original and is not under consideration elsewhere.
